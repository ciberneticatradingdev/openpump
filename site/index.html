Buyback share

The curve and the AMM route a configurable share of the protocol fee into buyback vaults. The vault authority spends it on $OPENPUMP. The hook is already in the code; the parameter is set on-chain.

30-day volume campaigns pay traders in a configured mint, across curve and AMM volume with one claim. $OPENPUMP is the natural reward.

Open by design

Not "trust us". Verifiable.

Multisig from day one

Upgrade authority of every program goes to a Squads multisig before mainnet. Signers are published. No single key can change the code.

Verified builds

Reproducible builds checked with solana-verify. The hash on-chain matches the commit in the repo, and you can check it yourself.

Nothing to drain

Protocol, creator and buyback fees leave the pool on every trade. There is no pile of fees sitting inside the contracts waiting for a sweep.

Audit before size

Not audited yet, and we say so. An external audit and a bug bounty are Phase 5, funded by the treasury, before anyone is asked to hold size here.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Why launch $OPENPUMP on pump.fun instead of your own curve?

Because the audience is there, and because a launchpad that nobody has used yet should not ask people to trust its contracts with their first purchase. The token validates demand and funds the mainnet deployment; the curve goes live in Phase 3 once the AMM has been running in public.

Is it a copy of pump.fun's code?

No. The programs were written from pump.fun's public behaviour: IDLs, on-chain events, and the community's bytecode analysis. No code was copied; seeds, authorities, account layouts and fee math are our own. The repo keeps a feature-by-feature parity matrix so you can see exactly what matches, what differs and what we left out on purpose.

Is it audited?

Not yet. It has 54 integration tests and math unit tests, which is not the same thing. An external audit and a bug bounty are on the roadmap and funded by the treasury. Until then, treat every deployment as experimental.

Who controls the programs?

Right now, the developer wallet on devnet. Before any mainnet deployment the upgrade authority moves to a Squads multisig with published signers, and every upgrade is a verified build announced in advance.

Where do the fees go?

On the curve: 0.5 % to the protocol and 0.5 % to the coin's creator, from the very first trade. On the AMM: 0.20 % to liquidity providers, 0.05 % protocol, 0.05 % creator. A configurable share of the protocol fee goes to buyback vaults that buy $OPENPUMP. All of it is set on-chain and readable by anyone.

Can I run my own frontend or indexer?

Yes. The SDK, the indexer and the web app are in the repo. Point them at the program IDs and an RPC. That is the point.
